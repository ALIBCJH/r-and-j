# Situation Assessment & Prioritization — 2026-07-09

**R&J Interiors · Executive Intelligence System**
Status: Executive brief for CEO decision · Version 1.0 · Owner: Executive Intelligence System (CSO/Chief of Staff)

> **What this is.** The first full run of the Executive Loop (Observe → Analyze → Prioritize → Delegate → Review → Document → Improve). Its purpose is not to summarise — it is to force a single decision: **what is the one thing R&J should do next, and what should it deliberately not do.** It coordinates the existing departments (Company Brain, Capital Brain) and inherits their evidence. It changes no mission and no principle.

---

## 1. Observe — the company as it actually is

**What exists and is strong:**
- A resolved **identity** (a *trust* company, not a tech company) and eight principles — Company Brain Phase 01.
- Four rigorous strategic phases (Foundation, Customer, Market, Business Model) and a complete **Capital Brain** (operating system for funding). This is unusually deep thinking for the stage.
- A **shipped web visualizer** and a live pre-launch deposit campaign.
- Two complementary founders: Rose (design, woman co-founder) + Simon (engineering, technical co-founder).

**What exists and is weak — and it is the same thing four times:**
1. **Zero customer evidence.** No structured interviews have ever been run; every persona and willingness-to-pay claim is a labelled hypothesis (Phase 02 §1, §2).
2. **The core promise is unkept.** The company sells *"see it in your actual room,"* but the shipped Studio **re-tints stock scenes and does not composite curtains onto the customer's photo** (Phase 02 §0.2.1). For a company whose entire moat is trust, *this is the most dangerous fact in the company.*
3. **Demand is unproven.** The deposit is refundable → it measures curiosity, not intent (Phase 01 §0.2.3). "Spots taken" is not traction.
4. **The economics are unmeasured.** True per-order margin and fulfilment capacity are unknown (Phase 04; Phase 03 §0.3).

Plus two fast-fix credibility leaks that actively corrode the trust thesis: the **~2× homepage/catalog price contradiction** and the **overstated shipped-VR claim** (Phase 01 §0.2.1–2).

## 2. Analyze — what the observation means

The departments were each told to flag their top gap. **They independently converged on one root cause:** R&J is making consequential, compounding decisions — product roadmap, pricing, GTM, fundraising narrative, capital sequencing — on top of a foundation that is *assumed true but neither validated nor kept.*

This is the classic, expensive failure mode for a strong team: **the strategy outruns the evidence.** The company has written ~40,000 words of first-principles strategy and held zero customer conversations. Each new strategic document currently *increases confidence without increasing knowledge* — which, for a company that sells honesty, is a principle violation (Principle 4: evidence beats opinion), not just an inefficiency.

The corollary is uncomfortable but clarifying: **the marginal strategic document is now worth less than the marginal customer conversation.** More brains will not make R&J more valuable right now. Evidence will.

## 3. Prioritize — the single highest-leverage objective

> **THE ONE THING: run a "Prove the Core" sprint — validate the customer and make the product tell the truth — before adding any more strategy, spending on paid acquisition, or opening equity conversations.**

It bundles the smallest set of actions that share one root and unlock the most downstream value:

**A. Validate the customer (the evidence).**
- 15 structured interviews with real target homeowners (Phase 02 §12 has the instrument).
- Answer the load-bearing unknowns: Is the pain *agony or mild anxiety* (painkiller vs vitamin)? Will she pay a premium for certainty? How does she discover a curtain maker? What triggers the purchase?

**B. Make the product tell the truth (the trust).**
- Fix the **price contradiction** (a bait-and-switch signal for a trust company) — small.
- Fix the **VR overclaim** copy to match what ships — small.
- **Decide, don't yet build, the "actual room" compositing** — scope it, but gate the build on what A proves customers will pay for.

**C. Instrument reality (the numbers).**
- Export order data from KV; compute true **per-order margin** and **fulfilment capacity** (Phase 04 §11).
- Convert the refundable deposit into an **intent-measuring instrument**.

### Why this and not something else — the decision framework, applied
| Test | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Increases customer trust? | **Yes** — directly closes the unkept-promise and the credibility leaks |
| Solves a real customer problem? | **It verifies whether we are** — the prerequisite to solving one honestly |
| Strengthens competitive advantage? | **Yes** — the moat *is* trust; a broken promise is anti-moat |
| Aligns with the mission? | **Yes** — Principles 3, 4, 8 (build on verified problems; evidence beats opinion; question assumptions) |
| Still matters in 5 years? | **Yes** — every future decision compounds off whether the core thesis is true |

It passes all five. The alternative — continue building the strategic superstructure — fails test 2 and test 4.

### What R&J should deliberately NOT do now (the discipline)
- **Do not** write Company Brain Phases 05–13 yet.
- **Do not** stand up the Marketing / Operations / Finance / Engineering brains yet. *(They will be materially better written on evidence; building them now risks six documents anchored to a possibly-false premise.)*
- **Do not** spend on paid acquisition or open equity conversations until the product keeps its promise and diligence won't expose the gaps.
- **Do** claim the free capital in parallel (cloud credits, MSEA/KCEA registration) — zero-cost, non-distracting, unblocks later (Capital Brain).

## 4. Delegate — assignments by department

| Department | Assignment (this sprint) | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer Intelligence** *(to stand up)* | Run 15 interviews (Phase 02 §12 instrument); answer painkiller-vs-vitamin + WTP + discovery + trigger | An evidence memo that upgrades Phase 02 from hypothesis to findings |
| **Product / Engineering** | Fix price bug + VR copy now; **scope** (not build) actual-room compositing; export order data | Shipped fixes; a compositing build-decision brief; a data export |
| **Finance / Operations** | From the export: true per-order margin + fulfilment capacity; design the intent instrument | Grade-A unit economics; an intent-measuring deposit |
| **Capital Brain** | In parallel, claim free credits + close M0 (compliance) — no new fundraising asks until the core is proven | Credits secured; compliance closed |
| **Company Brain** | Hold Phases 05–13; stand ready to ingest interview findings into Phase 02/06 | (Paused by design) |
| **Executive System** | Coordinate; update the [Dashboard](executive-dashboard.md); re-prioritise on interview results | Updated dashboard; next-loop brief |

## 5. Review — the quality bar for the sprint's outputs
- The interview memo must **state which hypotheses survived and which died**, not just summarise conversations.
- The economics must be **measured (grade-A)**, not modelled — or clearly labelled if still estimated.
- No output may re-introduce a claim the product doesn't keep (the trust standard applies to our own documents).

## 6. Document — what becomes permanent
- Interview findings → fold into **Phase 02** (upgrade its grade) and brief **Phase 06 (Product)**.
- Measured economics → unblock **Phase 04** and **Phase 10 (Finance)**.
- This assessment and the dashboard → the permanent Executive Intelligence layer.

## 7. Improve — the next highest-leverage action after this
Once the core is proven (or disproven):
- **If validated** (customer feels agony, pays premium, discovery/trigger clear): green-light the actual-room compositing build (Phase 06), then resume the strategy phases and open the strongest capital doors — now on evidence.
- **If disproven** (vitamin, not painkiller; won't pay premium): this is the cheapest, most valuable failure the company will ever buy. Pivot the thesis *before* pouring more capital and strategy into it. **That optionality is the entire point of doing this first.**

## The recommendation, in one line

**Freeze the superstructure, run the "Prove the Core" sprint, and let evidence — not another document — decide what R&J builds next.** The two founder inputs that gate the timeline (runway, compliance) should be answered this week (Capital Brain §9).
