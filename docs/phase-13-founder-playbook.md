## 1. The Operating Cadence (how the manual becomes a habit)

A strategy manual dies if it isn't wired to a rhythm. R&J's rhythm — deliberately light for two people:

| Cadence | Ritual | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | Fulfilment first. The business is *made, delivered, installed* — protect the craft hours. | Phase 08 |
| **Weekly (30 min)** | The *one priority*: are we advancing the current milestone's single objective? Kill or defer anything that isn't it. | Phase 12 §1 |
| **Fortnightly (to launch)** | Roadmap check: current stage exit criteria; re-sequence only if a dependency moved. | Phase 12 §6 |
| **Monthly (1 hr)** | The **triple close**: financial close (runway/margin — Phase 10 §6) + risk review (§6 indicators — Phase 11 §7) + capital register re-score (Capital Brain). One sitting, three lenses. | P10·P11·Capital |
| **Quarterly (½ day)** | Manual re-audit: revisit `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, update phases with new evidence, confirm no Non-Negotiable drifted. | Phase 01 §Amendment |

**The discipline:** these are *short* and *outcome-checked*, not status theatre. If a ritual can't be done in its time-box, the manual is too heavy — simplify it, don't skip it.

## 2. Division of Decision Rights (who owns what)

Two founders scale only if they **stop deciding everything together.** The split, by domain and by the Phase 08 §3 trust-vs-standard logic:

- **Rose owns:** design judgment, fabric/aesthetic decisions, QC sign-off (Phase 08 §2), the customer relationship and consultation, brand voice, content, and referral relationships. *The taste and trust layer.*
- **Simon owns:** the platform, data/instrumentation (Phase 07), the financial model and close (Phase 10), pricing mechanics, ops systems, and capital execution. *The systems and money layer.*
- **Joint (both, in writing):** anything one-way-door — brand promises, pricing *architecture*, hiring, taking capital, entering B2B/H2/H3, and any change to a Non-Negotiable Principle (Phase 01 §13, §Amendment).

**The decision protocol (Phase 01 §13 made operational):**
- **Two-way door** (reversible): the domain owner decides *fast, alone*, and reports it. No committee.
- **One-way door** (costly to reverse): both founders, in writing, run the Four Gates, sleep on it. Slow is correct here.
- **Tie-breaker when they disagree:** *choose what a trusted company would choose, not a fast-growing one* (Phase 01 §13). If still deadlocked, the domain owner's call stands for two-way doors; one-way doors escalate to the founders' agreement's deadlock clause (§4).

**The scale metric for this section is the founder-touch ratio** (Phase 08 §6): the goal is for *standard-executing* decisions to leave the founders' desks entirely over time, while *trust-defining* ones never do.

## 3. How to Say No (protecting the scarcest resource)

R&J's binding constraint is not capital — it is **founder attention.** The manual's non-goals and deferrals are therefore a standing **"no" list**, and saying no on time is the highest-leverage founder skill:
- **No** to features that name no uncertainty (Phase 06 §7), growth spend before the keystones (Phase 11 governance law), equity before M4 (Phase 12 §5), H2 before H1 trust metrics, and any "tech costume" spectacle (Phase 05).
- **No** to grant-chasing that outweighs customer work (Capital doctrine — the "grant-preneur trap").
- **The rule:** every yes to something off the critical path (Phase 12 §2) is a no to the one priority. Make that trade *consciously*, not by drift.

## 4. Founder-Level Commitments (the decisions the whole manual deferred to here)

Two artifacts every prior phase pointed at, and that only the founders can produce:

**(a) The Founders' Agreement (Phase 11 R2 — the master structural mitigation).** Before any capital, execute a written agreement covering: **equity split + vesting**, roles/decision rights (§2), **deadlock resolution**, buyout/exit, **IP assignment to the company**, time commitment, and compensation. A company literally named "R&J" cannot have this be tacit. *This is the single most important non-product action in the manual.*

**(b) The Five Blocking Decisions (Capital Brain §9).** The capital sequence cannot finalise until the founders answer, in writing:
1. **Real runway** (Phase 10 §1) — cash ÷ burn, in months.
2. **Dilution appetite** — how much of R&J are they willing to sell, ever, and for what?
3. **Compliance status** (Capital G4) — registered? KRA/MSEA current?
4. **Founder time budget** — realistic hours for M0–M2 *without* dropping fulfilment (§1).
5. **Equity floor** — the ownership % below which they will not go.

**Decision:** these seven items (agreement + five decisions) are a **founder offsite deliverable in M0** — cheap, founder-only, and unblocking of the entire capital and hiring sequence. Nothing downstream is truly safe until they exist.

## 5. Keeping the Manual Alive (anti-shelfware rules)

A Company Brain is worthless the day it's out of date. The rules that keep it living:
- **`[ASSUMPTION — verify]` tags are a backlog, not decoration.** Each is a debt to retire; the quarterly re-audit (§1) burns them down as real data arrives (especially the grade-C personas, margins, and market numbers in Phases 02/03/04/10).
- **Evidence updates the doc.** When M2 interviews or M3 economics land, the source phase is *edited*, not left stale, and its dependents re-checked (the handoff notes in each phase say which).
- **Non-Negotiables change only by explicit, dated, written founder approval** (Phase 01 §Amendment). Everything else is revised freely. This is what makes the eight principles a *constitution*, not a mood.
- **One source of truth per question:** strategy → the relevant phase; "what now" → Phase 12; "how we raise" → Capital Brain; "how we operate" → this phase. No duplicating; cross-reference.

## 6. Founder Sustainability (the human layer no other phase owns)

The manual's entire thesis rests on **trust that compounds slowly** (Phase 01 Belief 2) — which means the founders must still be standing in five years. The under-discussed risk (Phase 11 R2) is not that R&J fails fast but that its two people **burn out** running a founder-bound service while also building a company:
- **Protect the craft, then protect the founders.** The productisation plan (Phase 08 §3) is not only a scale lever — it is a *sustainability* lever; every SOP that offloads a founder buys back the energy the long game needs.
- **Don't let the two-person intimacy become a two-person prison.** The brand sells "the founders in every order" (Phase 05) — but the roadmap deliberately loosens that (founder-touch ratio) so the founders can lead without being consumed.
- **The pace is a marathon, deliberately.** "Slowly, then all at once" (Phase 01 §1) is also permission to *not* sprint into burnout for a quarter's numbers (Principle 2: trust over growth applies to the founders' own capacity too).

## Executive Summary (one page)

The first twelve phases built a company's mind; this final phase builds the **operators' operating system** — how two people actually run all of it without dropping the fulfilment that keeps the lights on. Its premise is that a strategy manual dies unless it is wired to a *rhythm* and a *division of labour*, so it installs both, deliberately light: a weekly one-priority check, a fortnightly roadmap review to launch, and a single monthly "triple close" that does finance, risk, and capital in one sitting — plus a quarterly re-audit that keeps the manual honest by burning down its own `[ASSUMPTION]` tags as real evidence arrives.

The heart of the playbook is **decision rights**, because two founders scale only when they stop deciding everything together. Rose owns the taste-and-trust layer (design, QC sign-off, customer, brand, referral); Simon owns the systems-and-money layer (platform, data, model, pricing mechanics, capital). Anything one-way-door — brand promises, pricing architecture, hiring, capital, H2/H3, or a change to a Non-Negotiable — is joint and in writing. The protocol operationalises Phase 01's reversibility test: reversible calls are made fast by the domain owner alone; irreversible ones are made slowly, together, through the Four Gates, with the standing tie-breaker — *choose what a trusted company would choose, not a fast-growing one.* And because the binding constraint on R&J is not capital but **founder attention**, the manual's non-goals become a standing "no" list: every yes off the critical path is a conscious no to the one priority.

The playbook then forces the two founder-level commitments the whole manual deferred to here: a **founders' agreement** (equity/vesting, roles, deadlock, buyout, IP) — the single most important non-product action in the company, and the legal substrate of its biggest structural risk — and the **five blocking decisions** (runway, dilution appetite, compliance, time budget, equity floor) without which the capital sequence cannot finalise. Both are a cheap, founder-only M0 offsite. Finally, it adds the human layer no other phase owns: the thesis depends on trust that compounds *slowly*, which means the founders must still be standing in five years — so productisation is reframed as a *sustainability* lever, the two-person intimacy is deliberately loosened so it doesn't become a two-person prison, and the marathon pace is treated as permission, not failure. The manual closes where it opened: R&J removes the fear from changing your home, the moat is trust, the wedge is curtains, the product is certainty, and the promise is *you will not be surprised.* Everything else is just doing that, in order, without burning out.

## Key Decisions
1. **Adopt the light operating cadence** (weekly priority · fortnightly roadmap · monthly triple-close · quarterly re-audit) as the mechanism that keeps the manual alive.
2. **Ratify the decision-rights split** (Rose: trust layer · Simon: systems layer · joint: one-way doors) and the reversible-fast / irreversible-slow protocol.
3. **Execute the Founders' Agreement in M0** — equity/vesting/roles/deadlock/buyout/IP — before any capital.
4. **Answer the five blocking decisions in writing** at an M0 founder offsite; the capital sequence stays parked until they exist.
5. **Treat the non-goals as a standing "no" list** and protect founder attention as the scarcest resource.
6. **Keep the manual living:** `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are a backlog; evidence edits the source phase; Non-Negotiables change only by dated written approval.
7. **Manage for founder sustainability** — use productisation to buy back founder energy; run the marathon, not the sprint.

## Open Questions
- **Will the founders actually hold the cadence** under fulfilment pressure? The honest failure risk of any playbook; revisit at the first quarterly re-audit.
- **Are the decision-rights boundaries right**, or will real friction redraw them? Treat §2 as v1, adjust with evidence.
- **What is each founder's true time budget** (blocking decision #4) — and does it allow M0–M2 in parallel, or must the schedule stretch?
- **Who is the tie-breaking third voice** (advisor/mentor/board) if the founders deadlock on a one-way door before the agreement's clause is tested? Consider naming one now.
- **What does "done" look like for the whole program?** Likely never — the manual is a living system; the real question is whether the *rituals* (§1) persist after the novelty fades.
