Status: Draft for founder approval · Version 1.0 · 2026-07-09
Owner: Founders (Rose Kabathi, Simon Juma) · Review cadence: Annually, or on entry to a new market/horizon

> **What this document is.** An honest sizing and structural map of the market R&J plays in — how big it is, how it is shaped, who else is in it, why now is the right time, and, most importantly, **where a venture-scale outcome can and cannot come from.** It inherits the customer segments from [Phase 02 — Customer](phase-02-customer.md) §4 and the positioning from [Phase 01 — Foundation](phase-01-foundation.md), and it deliberately confronts the central tension the [Strategy Review](strategy-review-phase1.md) raised: *a great regional business and a venture outcome are not the same market, and R&J's marketing has been sizing the wrong one.*
>
> **Evidence grade of this document: B/C (triangulated estimate).** Population and household counts are grade-A (census / World Bank). Market-value figures are grade-B (third-party analyst estimates — Statista, Ken Research, 6Wresearch — which disagree with each other and should be read as order-of-magnitude, not precise). The bottoms-up model is grade-C: a transparent chain of labelled assumptions, every one of which is a knob you can turn. **The purpose is not a precise number; it is a defensible order of magnitude and a clear answer to "is this venture-scale, and if so, through which door?"** All estimates are dated July 2026.

### 0.1 Current-state audit (what R&J currently believes about its market)
- The **marketing** positions R&J against a large, tech-flavoured market ("redefine how spaces are designed and experienced") — an implicitly billion-dollar framing.
- The **product** (custom curtains, KSh 4,500–10,000/panel, Nairobi + Nyeri) serves a **narrow, premium slice of the Kenyan window-treatment market** — a low-tens-of-millions-USD served market at most (sized in §3).

### 0.2 Contradictions found (tracked to owners)
1. **The market being *sold* is not the market being *served*.** The pitch implies a huge tech TAM; the served market is premium custom curtains in two Kenyan regions — **single-digit-millions USD annually at realistic capture** (§3). This is the identity gap from the Strategy Review, now *quantified*. **Owner: Founders/Brand — the pitch must size the market R&J is actually in (H1) while being honest that venture scale lives in H2/H3.**

3. **"Market size for whom?"** The tech ambition's TAM (a software/platform market) and the curtain business's TAM (a furnishings retail market) are **different markets with different sizes, buyers, and multiples.** Conflating them (as the marketing does) produces a number that is either dishonestly large or discouragingly small depending on which the reader assumes. This document sizes **both, separately** (§3, §9).

## 1. Executive Summary

R&J plays in the **Kenyan home-furnishings market**, entering through its **window-treatment (curtains & blinds) segment** — a fragmented, informal-dominated category with **no trusted, branded, made-to-order national player.** That absence is the opportunity.

**The numbers, honestly:**
- The **served market for premium, custom curtains** in R&J's target income band, nationally, is roughly **KSh 2.5–3.5 billion/year (~USD 19–27m)** — a real but modest market (§3). Concentrated in Nairobi + Central (R&J's beachhead), the reachable slice (SAM) is **~KSh 0.9–1.2bn/year (~USD 7–9m)**.
- At two-founder atelier capacity, near-term realistic revenue (SOM) is **single-digit-millions KSh/year** — bounded not by demand but by **fulfilment capacity** (the scalability ceiling the Strategy Review flagged).

**The conclusion that must not be softened:** *the founding curtain business, however excellent, is a good regional business — not a venture-scale one.* Venture scale, if R&J wants it, lives in one of three deliberately-sequenced expansions, each a **materially larger market**:
- **H2 — Own the room:** the full **soft-furnishings + décor** market (Home Décor alone ~USD 155m and growing; Furniture & Interiors ~USD 1.2bn) — 5–50× the curtain wedge, same customer, same promise.
- **H3a — B2B:** developers, hospitality, staged real estate — higher AOV, repeat buyers, the antidote to the once-a-decade homeowner.
- **H3b — Platform (Door B):** licensing the certainty engine to *other makers* — a market sized by every maker's GMV, not R&J's own retail. The only genuinely venture-scale door, and the one that (per Phase 01 §14) requires a deliberate identity decision.

**The market timing is genuinely favourable** (§7): urbanisation past 30% and rising, a growing (if contested) middle class, a national housing push, and near-universal M-Pesa/mobile penetration that makes a digital-preview + local-fulfilment model newly viable. **The right read is: enter a small market you can dominate on trust, then ride the customer relationship into the large adjacent markets — exactly the Foundation's sequence, now with numbers under it.**

## 2. Market Definition — What Market Are We Actually In?

Getting the definition right is worth more than getting the number precise, because the definition decides which number matters.

**The category R&J competes in (supply-side):** the **Kenyan home-furnishings market**, specifically the **custom/made-to-order window-treatments segment** (curtains, sheers, blinds, drapery), sold with **design consultation + delivery + installation**.

**The job the market actually buys (demand-side, from Phase 02 §3):** not "curtains" but **certainty on an expensive, irreversible home decision.** This matters for sizing: R&J does not compete for *all* curtain spend — it competes for the spend of customers who will pay a premium to *not get it wrong* (Phase 02 §7). That is a **premium slice**, not the whole category.

**Three concentric markets — name them so nobody conflates them:**
1. **The wedge (H1, today):** premium custom curtains, Nairobi + Central. *Small, ownable, the credibility deposit.*
2. **The adjacency (H2):** all made-to-order soft furnishings + home décor for the same customer. *Large, same trust engine.*
3. **The platform / B2B (H3):** the certainty engine sold to other makers, and B2B channels. *Largest, different customer, different company (Phase 01 §14 documented conflict).*

**The definitional discipline:** when R&J says "our market," it must say *which* of these three. The Strategy Review's 4.5/10 was largely the penalty for answering "the big one" while operating in "the small one."

### 3.1 Bottoms-up (the primary model)

| Step | Value | Basis / grade |
|---|---|---|
| Kenya households | ~13.0M | A — 12.14M in 2019 census, grown at pop. rate |
| Urban households (~30%) | ~3.9M | A — World Bank urbanisation 30% |
| R&J's income band (top ~15–20% of urban HH by income — "considered-purchase" ability, Phase 02 §4) | ~600,000 HH | C — matches Strategy Review's ~500k estimate |
| Buying in a given year (curtains once / ~6 yrs) | ~100,000 HH/yr | C — frequency assumed |

| **TAM — national premium custom curtains/yr** | **~KSh 3.0bn (~USD 23m)** | **C** |
| **SAM — Nairobi + Central (~35% of band)** | **~KSh 1.05bn (~USD 8m)** | **C** |
| **SOM — 2-founder atelier at capacity (~180–240 orders/yr × KSh 30k)** | **~KSh 5.4–7.2m/yr (~USD 42–56k)** | **C — capacity-bound, verify §0.3** |

The **SOM is the honest headline for the near term**: R&J's constraint today is not demand — it is **how many orders two founders can measure, make, and install without breaking the intimacy they sell** (Phase 02 §6 Stage 5; Strategy Review scalability = 3/10). Growing SOM toward SAM requires *productising fulfilment* (Phase 08 Operations), not more marketing.

### 3.2 Top-down cross-check

- **Kenya Home Décor market ≈ USD 155m** (2029, Statista) → curtains & blinds subsegment ≈ 15–20% → **~USD 23–31m** → premium/custom slice ≈ 50–70% → **~USD 12–22m**.
- This **brackets the bottoms-up TAM (~USD 23m)** from below — consistent order of magnitude. The two methods agreeing at **~USD 15–25m national premium-curtain TAM** is the most trustworthy claim in this section.
- **Broader context ceiling:** Kenya Furniture & Interiors ≈ **USD 1.2bn** (Ken Research) — this is the *H2 adjacency* ceiling, not R&J's curtain market. It is the number that matters for the expansion thesis (§9), and it is **~50× the curtain wedge.**

### 3.3 What the sizing means
- **H1 (curtains) is demonstrably not venture-scale.** ~USD 15–25m national TAM, single-digit-millions reachable, capacity-bound capture. A *very good* business; not a $1B one. This is not a failure — it is the correct wedge (highest-regret purchase, Phase 01 §4). But it must be *named* as a wedge.
- **The size argument for R&J is a *sequencing* argument, not a *TAM* argument.** The curtain market is big enough to build a trusted, profitable, referral-compounding brand — and that brand is the key that unlocks the 50×-larger adjacent market (H2) and the different-market platform (H3). **R&J's market is small today by design and large tomorrow by sequence.**

## 4. Market Structure & Value Chain

**Structure: highly fragmented, informal-dominated, no trusted branded leader.**

The Kenyan window-treatment market is a long tail of:
- **Informal tailors & WhatsApp sellers** — the volume majority; compete on price and existing personal trust; no design assurance, no outcome guarantee, variable quality.
- **Fabric shops / market stalls** (e.g. Eastleigh, Nairobi CBD) — sell material, outsource making; the customer bears all integration risk.
- **Online marketplaces (Jumia, Kilimall)** — commodity, ready-made, no customisation or install; race to the bottom.
- **A thin layer of premium interior-design studios** — serve the wealthy, project-based, expensive, no productised preview; R&J's closest quality peers but a different (higher, narrower) price point.
- **Global visualization tech** (Havenly, IKEA Kreativ, Modsy [defunct], Shopify AR apps) — depict but do not fulfil *in Kenya*; not local competitors, but they commoditise the *visualizer* R&J must not over-rely on (Strategy Review §4).

**The structural insight:** the market is fragmented precisely because **trust does not scale in it** — every seller rebuilds trust one relationship at a time, and no one has packaged trust into a *brand* that travels. **That gap — a trusted, branded, made-to-order national player — is the whitespace R&J is built to fill** (Phase 01 moat: local craft trust + vertical integration). Fragmentation is both the opportunity (no incumbent to displace) and the threat (low barriers, easy price competition at the bottom).

**Value chain — R&J's position:** most players occupy *one* link (sell fabric, *or* sew, *or* deliver). R&J occupies the **whole chain** (design → make → deliver → install), which is what lets it *guarantee the outcome* and capture full margin (Phase 01 §9.2). Vertical integration is R&J's structural bet against a fragmented, hand-off-prone market.

## 5. Competitive Landscape

Detailed competitive analysis lives in the [Strategy Review §4](strategy-review-phase1.md); here we position players by the two axes that decide R&J's fate — **trust/quality** vs **price** — and name who R&J actually races.

- **Real competitor today = the status quo:** WhatsApp sellers, tailors, fabric shops. They win on **price, speed, and pre-existing personal trust.** R&J beats them only on **certainty + guaranteed outcome + brand** — never on price (Phase 02 §7, §8.3).
- **Aspirational peers = premium interior studios.** Higher price, project-based, no productised preview. R&J can undercut them on price *and* out-assure them on preview — a genuine wedge for the Proud Upgrader (Phase 02 §4).
- **The visualizer is not a moat here.** Every global AR/AI tool commoditises it. R&J's defensibility is the *welding* of preview to local fulfilment and trust, which none of the tech players have in Kenya (Strategy Review §4 "invert it").
- **The instructive death: Modsy.** Well-funded, did visualization *without* fulfilment, died. The market lesson R&J must internalise: **in this category, fulfilment is the moat and visualization is the hook — not the reverse.**

**Barrier-to-entry reality:** the *software* barrier is ~a weekend (low). The *trust + integrated-fulfilment + local-brand* barrier is years (high). R&J must compete on the high-barrier axis and treat the low-barrier one as table stakes.

## 6. Market Trends & Dynamics

Forces shaping the market over the next 3–5 years, with R&J's exposure to each:

1. **Urbanisation (tailwind).** ~30% urban and rising; urban households are R&J's whole market. More urban homes = more windows to dress with disposable income.
2. **Middle-class growth (mixed).** Real but *fragile* — much of Kenya's "middle class" is a "floating" band that can slip back (World Bank). **Implication: R&J should anchor on the *established* upper-middle and Proud Upgrader (Phase 02 §4), not the fragile floating band whose premium spend is first cut in a downturn.**
3. **Housing supply push (tailwind, capped).** Government targets 250k affordable units/yr; actual delivery ~50k/yr against 150–200k demand. Even under-delivered, every new home is a **trigger event** (Phase 02 §6 Stage 1) — but affordable-housing buyers skew below R&J's premium band, so the relevant tailwind is the *market-rate* new-build and renovation segment, not the affordable programme.
4. **Digital + M-Pesa ubiquity (structural enabler).** Near-universal mobile money and rising smartphone penetration are precisely what make a *digital-preview + local-fulfilment + mobile-deposit* model viable now in a way it was not a decade ago. This is the "why now" (§7).
5. **Rising interior-design awareness (tailwind).** Social media (Instagram, Pinterest, local design influencers) is manufacturing taste-aspiration — expanding the Proud Upgrader and Social-Job segments (Phase 02 §3, §4).
6. **Commoditising AI visualization (headwind to the tech story).** Generative "reimagine your room" tools are crowding and near-free — which *erodes the tech-differentiation claim* and *reinforces* that R&J's edge must be fulfilment + trust, not the visualizer (Phase 01 §10).

---

## 7. Why Now

The market timing argument — why R&J is neither too early nor too late:

- **Not too early:** M-Pesa, smartphones, and social-driven design aspiration have *just* reached the penetration where a digital-preview commerce model works for the mass-affluent Kenyan home — the infrastructure R&J rides did not exist at this level a decade ago.
- **Not too late:** despite that infrastructure, **no one has built the trusted, branded, made-to-order national player** (§4). The whitespace is still open. The global visualization players have retreated or died; the local market is still fragmented and informal.
- **The closing window:** AR/AI visualization is commoditising fast (§6.6). The *tool* advantage has a short shelf life — which means R&J's window to convert a temporary tool-lead into a *durable trust-and-fulfilment brand* is **now**, before the tool is table stakes everywhere. **Speed on the moat (trust, fulfilment, brand), patience on the tool.**

## 9. The Venture-Scale Question — Confronted Directly

This is the section the Strategy Review demands and the marketing avoids.

**Is R&J venture-scale?** *In curtains: no.* ~USD 15–25m national TAM, capacity-bound capture, once-a-decade frequency (§3). A curtain-only R&J is a **cash-generative regional SME**, fundable by an angel or a creative-economy grant (see `docs/funding-shortlist.md`), not a venture fund expecting a $1B outcome.

**Where scale actually lives — three doors, sized:**
- **Door H2 (own the room):** the ~USD 1.2bn furniture-&-interiors / ~USD 155m décor market. Same trust engine, same customer, larger basket, better LTV (fixes the once-a-decade problem). **Most natural, lowest-identity-risk path.** Realistic *venture-adjacent* scale.
- **Door H3a (B2B):** developers/hospitality — repeat, high-AOV, lower-CAC. Smaller count of buyers, much larger orders. A pragmatic scale-lever and a possible *better* beachhead than homeowners — **worth one live experiment now** (Strategy Review Rec. 8).
- **Door H3b (platform):** the only *true* venture market — sized by the GMV of every maker who runs on R&J's stack (the "Shopify for made-to-order furnishings" thesis, Strategy Review §5 Door B). Largest, most defensible (network + data), **but a different company** that partially conflicts with Principle 3 and requires explicit founder approval (Phase 01 §14 documented conflict).

**The honest strategic statement for investors and founders alike:**
> *R&J is entering a small, ownable market (premium Kenyan curtains) to build the one asset that is hard to manufacture — trust — and using it as the key to markets 5–50× larger (all home furnishings) or structurally different (a maker platform). The venture case is not "curtains are big." It is "trust, once earned in the highest-regret purchase, transfers — and the adjacent markets are large."*

That is a defensible venture narrative **because it is true.** The 4.5/10 was for telling a *different, false* version.

## 10. Market Risks

Ranked; several inherited from the Strategy Review §6 and re-cast as *market* (not execution) risks:

1. **Small served market for H1 (structural).** If H2/H3 expansion stalls, R&J is capped as a regional SME. *Mitigation: instrument H1 trust metrics early so H2 can be justified and funded.*
2. **Fragmented market = permanent price pressure at the bottom.** The informal tail keeps a cheap alternative always visible. *Mitigation: never compete there; own the trust/certainty axis (§5).*
3. **Middle-class fragility.** Premium home spend is discretionary and cyclical; a downturn hits R&J's band first. *Mitigation: anchor on established upper-middle + B2B (counter-cyclical-ish).*
4. **Tool commoditisation erodes the visible differentiator.** *Mitigation: shift the story to fulfilment + trust before the tool is free everywhere (§7).*
5. **A funded entrant could brand the whitespace first.** The whitespace R&J sees, others can see. *Mitigation: R&J's local-craft-founder trust is the slowest thing to copy — press that advantage now.*
6. **Geographic concentration.** Nairobi + Central dependence exposes R&J to local shocks. *Low near-term, rises with scale.*

## 11. Applying the Decision Framework to Market Bets

Phase 01 §13 Four Gates on the major market decisions:

- **Bet: "Dominate Nairobi + Central before expanding geographically."** All gates ✅; two-way door → **decide fast, execute.**
- **Bet: "Frame the investor story on H1 TAM alone."** Evidence Gate ❌ (H1 isn't venture-scale) and Long-Term Gate ❌ (undersells the real thesis) → **rejected; frame on the sequencing thesis (§9).**

- **Bet: "Open the platform (H3b) market now."** One-way door + Principle-3 conflict (Phase 01 §14) → **decide slowly, at founder level, only after H1 trust metrics prove the engine.**
