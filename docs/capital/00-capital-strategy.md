## 1. Executive summary

R&J is **pre-launch, pre-revenue**, women- and technically-co-founded, with a working web visualizer and a small (curiosity-grade, refundable) deposit campaign. In that position, the single most expensive mistake would be to **sell equity now** — into a weak story, at a punishing valuation, to fund a *service* business that is not itself venture-scale.

The strategy is therefore **non-dilutive-first, staged to milestones**:

1. **Fund the proof, not the ambition.** The near-term job of capital is not to scale — it is to **buy the evidence** (real customer validation, real unit economics, a real intent instrument) that makes every later door open wider and cheaper. Most of that costs founder time, not much money.
2. **Stack non-dilutive capital that also delivers validation and network** — grants, competitions, women-/technical-founder programs, cloud credits, and the Tony Elumelu-type fellowships. These pay in three currencies at once: cash, credibility, and connections. **This is the primary engine for the next 12 months.**
3. **Use debt, equipment finance, and customer financing** to fund the *physical and working-capital* layer (fabric, equipment, installment offers to customers) **without touching ownership** — but only once there is enough trading history to qualify and repay safely.
4. **Raise equity last and deliberately** — only when there is a genuine traction story, and only to buy the **H2/H3 platform ambition**, never to subsidise the H1 service margin. When we do, we raise on the *real* moat, not the tech costume.

**The governing metric is Expected Capital Value (ECV), not headline size.** A KSh 700K grant we will probably win, that also certifies us to the next funder, beats a KSh 20M equity long-shot that would cost 15% of the company and three months of founder attention. See [`02-scoring-model.md`](02-scoring-model.md).

## 3. Doctrine: why non-dilutive first (the true cost of capital)

The instinct of a pre-launch founder is to chase a VC cheque. That instinct is usually wrong at this stage. Rank the sources by **true cost per shilling raised**:

| Source | Cash cost | Ownership cost | Control cost | Hidden cost | Verdict at R&J's stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Grants / competitions** | 0% | 0% | 0% | Application effort; reporting | **Cheapest capital that exists.** Take first. |
| **Cloud/tool credits** | 0% | 0% | 0% | Almost none | **Free. Claim immediately.** |
| **Fellowships (TEF-type)** | 0% | 0% | 0% | Time in program | Cash + network + credibility. High ROI. |
| **Equipment / asset finance** | Interest | 0% | 0% | Collateral / repossession risk | Good — *once* there's an order pipeline to service the asset |
| **Working-capital debt** | Interest (can be high) | 0% | 0% | Personal guarantee risk | Use narrowly, only with reliable cashflow |
| **Customer financing** | Fee/discount | 0% | 0% | Default risk (usually borne by provider) | **A growth lever, not a fundraise** — expands AOV |
| **Revenue** | 0% | 0% | 0% | Slow | **The best capital of all.** Every shilling of margin is un-raisable equity you keep. |
| **Equity (pre-seed/angel)** | 0% now | **High — permanent** | Board/veto | Signalling; down-round risk if raised too high | **Most expensive. Last, and deliberately.** |

**Why equity is the most expensive money here:** a pre-launch valuation is a *floor*, not a ceiling. Selling 12–20% now — before the unit economics and validation that would 3–5× the valuation exist — permanently gives away the most ownership for the least cash. Every non-dilutive shilling raised in the meantime is a shilling that does *not* have to be bought with ownership, and it *raises* the eventual equity valuation by de-risking the story.

**The one legitimate reason to raise equity early:** if runway is about to end and no non-dilutive bridge exists, survival beats optimisation. That is a runway decision, and it makes stating **real current runway** (row above) the first input to this whole strategy.

## 5. Milestones that unlock capital (what each raise is *for*)

Capital is never raised in the abstract; it is raised against a milestone. R&J's capital-unlocking milestones:

1. **M0 — Fundable basics** (weeks): register/compliance current (KRA PIN, business reg), one-pager + data room v1, readiness gaps triaged. *Unlocks:* the ability to apply to anything at all.
2. **M1 — Real intent** (1–2 mo): the refundable deposit converted into an intent-measuring instrument; ≥ a credible number of committed pre-orders. *Unlocks:* the "validated demand" line every equity door asks for.
3. **M2 — Customer truth** (1–2 mo): 15–20 real customer interviews; personas and WTP moved from hypothesis to evidence (Phase 02 §12). *Unlocks:* credibility in interviews; sharper grant narratives.
4. **M3 — Measured economics** (post first orders): true gross margin per *order* (not panel), funnel instrumented, AOV known (Phase 04 §11). *Unlocks:* equipment/working-capital debt; angel conversations that survive diligence.
5. **M4 — Repeatable service** (6–12 mo of orders): a pipeline that runs without heroics; positive contribution margin. *Unlocks:* larger debt facilities; the credible pre-seed equity story.
6. **M5 — H2 wedge signal** (12–24 mo): first evidence that trust transfers beyond curtains (attach/own-the-room). *Unlocks:* the venture-scale equity narrative and strategic/corporate partnerships.

> Each opportunity in the register is tagged to the earliest milestone at which it becomes winnable. Do not spend effort on an M4 door while sitting at M0.

## 7. Portfolio approach (how many doors at once)

Treat applications as a **portfolio with a hit-rate**, not a lottery ticket:
- Run **3–5 live applications at any time**, weighted toward high-ECV, high-probability, low-effort doors.
- **Reuse ruthlessly** — the one-pager, narrative, financial model, and data room ([`assets/`](assets/)) are built once and tailored per door. The marginal cost of application #4 should be a fraction of #1.
- **Sequence by deadline, not by preference** — a strong door closing in 3 weeks beats a stronger door with a rolling window.
- **Kill fast** — if a door needs a milestone R&J will not hit before its deadline, park it in the register with the reason; don't let it consume attention.

## 8. Risks to the capital plan (and mitigations)

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Runway ends before non-dilutive capital lands** (grants are slow) | State real runway now; keep a minimal-viable equity/angel bridge identified as insurance; prioritise fast rolling programs & credits |
| **Over-raising / raising too early dilutes founders permanently** | The non-dilutive-first doctrine; raise equity only against M4+ milestones |
| **Diligence exposes ungraded unit economics / weak validation** | Close M2/M3 *before* equity conversations; never open a data room that undercuts the pitch |
| **Story inconsistency across applications** | Single source of narrative truth in [`assets/narrative-and-pitch-outline.md`](assets/narrative-and-pitch-outline.md) |
| **Grant dependence creates a "grant-preneur" trap** (chasing grants over customers)| Company Brain Principle 5 — solve real problems before chasing money; revenue is the primary fuel target by M4 |
| **Founder attention is the scarcest capital** | Portfolio cap of 3–5 live apps; reuse assets; the CRM enforces focus |
| **Compliance gates (KRA, registration) block government funds** | Close M0 basics first — cheap, and unblocks a whole tier of government capital |

## 9. Decisions the founders must make (blocking inputs)

These are founder decisions the Capital Strategy team cannot make alone; each changes the plan:

1. **Real runway** — how many months of cash at current burn? (Sets urgency, and whether an equity bridge is insurance or unnecessary.)
2. **Dilution appetite** — is R&J building for a venture outcome (then equity, eventually, is on the table) or an excellent cash-generative business (then stay non-dilutive far longer)? Company Brain leans the latter for H1, venture for H2/H3 — **confirm.**
3. **Compliance status** — are business registration, KRA PIN, and tax filings current? (Gate for all government funds.)
4. **Founder time budget** — how many hours/week can go to fundraising vs building? (Sets the portfolio cap.)
5. **Equity floor** — the maximum % the founders will part with in a first institutional round, and the minimum pre-money they'll accept. (Protects against a bad early raise.)

> Until #1 and #3 are answered, the register is directionally correct but the *sequence* cannot be finalised. **These are the first questions to close.**
