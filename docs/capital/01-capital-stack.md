## 2. Each type — what it is, R&J's angle, and the catch

### 1) Cloud / tool credits — *claim immediately*
Free compute and SaaS credits for startups. **R&J's angle:** the web/AR visualizer is real compute; credits directly subsidise it. **Catch:** almost none — a form and a pitch. **Sequence:** now. **Doors:** AWS Activate, Google for Startups Cloud, Microsoft for Startups, Nvidia Inception (see register).

### 2) Grants — *the primary engine for 12 months*
Non-dilutive cash, often with a mission/impact or creative-economy lens. **R&J's angle:** women-led + creative-tech + craft jobs + non-Nairobi. **Catch:** slow (3–6 mo), competitive, reporting overhead; a few are cohort-gated and go stale. **Sequence:** now, continuously. **Doors:** creative-economy funds, women-founder grants, DFI/British Council creative programs.

### 3) Innovation challenges / competitions — *cash + brand + network*
Pitch competitions and prizes. **R&J's angle:** women-founder tracks (AWIEF), creative-tech, African founder prizes. **Catch:** low hit-rate on the elite/global ones — treat as portfolio bets, not plans; the *value* is often the exposure as much as the cash. **Sequence:** now, selectively (high strategic multiplier, low effort ones).

### 4) Accelerators / incubators — *the right ones are free*
Structured programs: mentorship + network + (sometimes) a cheque. **R&J's angle:** the ideation-stage-friendly and women-in-tech programs. **Catch:** some take equity (weigh via the dilution factor); some need MVP+traction. Prefer **equity-free / ideation-stage** first. **Sequence:** now (timbuktoo, Women in Tech), later (equity-taking ones once there's traction to trade up on).

### 5) Government support — *cheap, but compliance-gated*
State funds and programs. **R&J's angle:** **Women Enterprise Fund** (0% Tuinuke tier) and **Hustler Fund** are among the *most pre-revenue-tolerant formal capital in Kenya*, especially organised via a women's group. **Catch:** most govt capital needs registration + KRA PIN + sometimes trading history; disbursement is bureaucratic. **Sequence:** now for WEF/Hustler (after M0 compliance); KIE machinery loan is a Year-2–3 target.

### 6) Fellowships (founder programs) — *cash + network + credibility*
Pan-African founder programs. **R&J's angle:** TEF-type non-dilutive seed + training; Rose/Simon unlock women-/technical-gated ones. **Catch:** annual cycles; competitive. **Sequence:** now, as windows open.

### 7) Customer financing — *not a fundraise; an AOV lever*
Let homeowners pay in installments so a KES 30–100k custom order clears the sticker-shock barrier. **R&J's angle:** **Safaricom Faraja** (0% to customer, R&J paid in full, zero credit risk to R&J — just a facility fee) as the primary rail; **Aspira** for true multi-month terms (up to 24 mo for furniture-class goods). **Catch:** needs an active Lipa na M-Pesa till + partner onboarding; **do not self-finance installments** out of deposits, and **do not depend on a single BNPL provider** (Lipa Later went into administration in 2025). **Sequence:** wire in at soft-launch; treat as a conversion feature, priced into quotes.

### 8) Revenue — *the best capital there is*
Every shilling of margin is un-raisable equity R&J keeps. **R&J's angle:** premium pricing on certainty; own-the-room AOV expansion (Phase 04). **Catch:** capacity-bound in H1 (two founders per order). **Sequence:** the primary fuel from launch; the whole point of M3–M4.

### 9) Equipment / asset finance — *the right way to buy machines*
Debt secured by the asset. **R&J's angle:** industrial sewing/curtain-making equipment via **KIE** (~10%, multi-year, machinery grace period) is the best-fit vehicle; **Watu** can finance a *delivery motorbike* even pre-trading (deposit + guarantors). **Catch:** KIE wants ~2 yrs trading + 140% collateral; **avoid personal logbook loans** for equipment (Mogo's regulatory fine is the cautionary tale). **Sequence:** delivery bike early if needed; machinery in Year 2–3.

### 10) Strategic / corporate partnerships — *fixes the LTV problem*
Money or in-kind from developers, hospitality, fabric suppliers, or AR/VR platforms. **R&J's angle:** B2B repeat high-AOV buyers (developers furnishing show-homes; hospitality soft furnishings), supply/trade-credit from textile partners, AR platform support for the visualizer. **Catch:** needs a product and reputation to partner around; long sales cycles. **Sequence:** deferred to M4–M5; the H2/H3 scale story.

### 11) Working-capital debt — *narrow, disciplined use*
Short-term cash against receivables/cashflow (Pezesha, 4G Capital, bank SME lines). **R&J's angle:** bulk fabric buys against confirmed orders once trading. **Catch:** **needs ~6 months of M-Pesa/business history**; monthly rates can compound to punishing APRs; personal-guarantee risk. **Sequence:** deferred to ~6–12 mo post-launch; use only against reliable cashflow.

### 12) Revenue-based financing — *later, revenue-gated*
Non-dilutive advances repaid as a % of revenue (Untapped, and the broader African RBF market). **R&J's angle:** non-dilutive growth capital once there's predictable monthly revenue. **Catch:** categorically requires recurring revenue; pre-revenue R&J cannot qualify. **Sequence:** revisit post-M4 if scaling non-dilutively.

### 13) Equity (angel / pre-seed VC) — *last, deliberate, for scale*
Selling ownership for cash. **R&J's angle:** Rose unlocks women-founder funds (FirstCheck), Simon unlocks technical-founder funds (Microtraction); the *real moat* (vertical integration + craft trust) is the pitch. **Catch:** the most expensive capital at this stage — a low pre-launch valuation permanently over-dilutes; diligence will expose ungraded unit economics and thin validation. **Sequence:** M4+, to fund the H2/H3 platform ambition — never to subsidise the H1 service margin.

---

## 3. The sequencing rule (one sentence)

**Take the free money now (credits), stack the cheap money next (grants, competitions, fellowships, women/govt funds), fund the physical layer with the right debt and customer-financing once trading, live on revenue as the primary fuel, and sell equity last — only for the scale ambition, only on a proven story, only at a fair price.**

---

## 4. What this means for the register

The [opportunity register](03-opportunity-register.md) is organised so that the **🟢 Act-now** section is dominated by types 1–6 (credits, grants, competitions, ideation accelerators, women/govt funds, fellowships); types 7–8 (customer financing, revenue) appear as **enablement plays** at soft-launch; and types 9–13 (equipment, partnerships, debt, RBF, equity) sit in **🔵 Watch**, tagged to the milestone that unlocks them.
