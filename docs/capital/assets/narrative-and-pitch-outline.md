## 1. The identity sentence (say this first, everywhere)

> **R&J is a trust company that removes the uncertainty from transforming your home — starting with the window, the highest-regret purchase — by letting you see your space before it's built, then delivering it with in-house craftsmanship.**

Not "a curtain company." Not "a VR startup." A **certainty engine** for home transformation, with the window as the wedge. (Company Brain Principle 1; Strategy Review identity resolution.)

**Do not** oversell "tech." The visualizer is *how* we build trust, not *what* we sell (Principle 3). Overselling technology invites the "good business in a tech costume" discount.

## 2. The narrative arc (the story, in order)

1. **The problem (real, expensive, universal).** Transforming a home is high-stakes and high-regret: you commit money to something you cannot see until it's too late to change. Curtains/soft furnishings are the sharpest version — a considered KES 30–100k decision made blind.
2. **Why now.** Affordable visualization + mobile money (M-Pesa) + a rising Kenyan middle class furnishing homes make it newly possible to *sell certainty* before production, on rails customers already trust.
3. **The solution.** See it (web visualizer) → perfect it → we build it (in-house, made-to-order) → we deliver & install it. One accountable party from preview to promise-kept.
4. **Why R&J wins (the real moat).** A compound no competitor holds together: **vertical integration** (design → make → deliver → install, capturing the whole margin) + **local craft trust** (founder-led, in-market) + a **designer-engineer founding team** + the **certainty engine**. Visualization alone is copyable (Modsy died doing it without fulfilment); the *integrated* whole is not.
5. **The team (the levers).** **Rose Kabathi** — designer, the taste and the customer trust (and a woman co-founder). **Simon Juma** — engineer, the product and the visualizer (a technical co-founder). Designer + engineer is exactly the pairing this problem needs.
6. **Traction (honest).** A working visualizer in market; a pre-launch deposit campaign. *[State only what is true and measured — see the honesty rules in §5. Upgrade this section as G1/G2/G3 close in [`04-readiness-gaps.md`](../04-readiness-gaps.md).]*
7. **The market & the climb.** H1 (curtains) is a healthy, cash-generative service — the trust-manufacturing engine, not the venture prize. The venture case is **trust transferring to adjacencies**: H2 own-the-room (basket expansion, higher AOV), H3 B2B (developers/hospitality — fixes the once-a-decade LTV problem) and ultimately a platform (software margins). *The money today is service; the money at scale is what the service earns the right to sell next.* (Phase 03 §9; Phase 04 §1.)
8. **The ask.** *[Tailored per door — the specific amount, use of funds, and milestone it buys. See §4.]*

## 4. The ask, by capital type (use-of-funds discipline)

Every ask names **the milestone it buys** ([`00-capital-strategy.md §5`](../00-capital-strategy.md)):

- **Grant/fellowship (~$5–10k):** funds M1–M2 — the intent instrument + customer-validation program + first production run. *"Non-dilutive capital to convert validated interest into proven paying demand."*
- **Cloud credits:** funds the visualizer's compute — *"scale the certainty engine without burning cash on infrastructure."*
- **Nyeri County EDF / equipment finance:** funds M3–M4 — machinery/worksheds to lift capacity beyond the two-founder ceiling. *"Break the capacity bound on a proven service."*
- **Customer financing (enablement):** not an ask — a *feature*; funds the customer's purchase, lifts AOV.
- **Equity (later, M4+):** funds the H2/H3 climb — *"we've proven trust converts and repeats; now fund the expansion from a service margin to a platform margin."* Never framed as funding the H1 service to survive.

## 5. Honesty rules (non-negotiable — the trust thesis applies to us too)

1. **Never present the refundable deposit as validated demand** until it measures committed intent (G2). Say "pre-launch interest," not "traction," until then.
2. **Never claim shipped VR/immersive capability** — only the web Studio ships (G8). Frame Quest VR as roadmap.
3. **Never quote unit economics as fact** until measured (G3). Say "modelled" and give the assumption.
4. **Never imply the product composites curtains onto the user's real room** if it re-tints stock scenes (G7). Describe what it does.
5. **Never quote a single price that contradicts the catalog** (G6 — fix the bug first).
6. **A company that sells trust cannot fundraise on exaggeration.** Every overclaim is a diligence landmine and a betrayal of the core thesis. Under-promise; let the real moat carry the pitch.
