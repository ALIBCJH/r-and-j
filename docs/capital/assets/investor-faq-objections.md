> **What this is.** The hard questions a sharp investor, grantor, or lender *will* ask — and honest, prepared answers. The rule: **answer the real question, don't deflect.** A company that sells trust cannot handle objections with spin. Where the honest answer exposes a gap, the answer is "here's the gap and here's what we're doing about it" — which is more credible than a dodge. Objections map to [`04-readiness-gaps.md`](../04-readiness-gaps.md).

## On the business model & scale

**Q: Isn't this just a curtain service business? Where's the venture scale?**
A: The curtain service is deliberately H1 — a cash-generative *trust engine*, not the prize. Two founders per order caps it at single-digit-millions KSh/year, and we don't pretend otherwise. The scale story is that the trust we earn on the highest-regret purchase (the window) transfers to adjacencies: own-the-room (higher AOV), then B2B with developers/hospitality (which fixes the once-a-decade repeat problem), then a platform. We're raising against *that* climb, not against curtains being big.

**Q: Visualization is commoditised — Modsy died. Why won't you?**
A: Modsy did visualization *without* fulfilment — it inspired demand it couldn't capture. Our moat is the opposite: we own design → make → deliver → install, so the visualizer isn't the product, it's how we build the trust that lets us capture the whole margin. The integration is the defensible part; the canvas re-tint is not, and we don't pretend it is.

**Q: What's the real moat?**
A: A compound: vertical integration + local founder-led craft trust + a designer-engineer team + the certainty engine. No single element is unique; holding all four together, in-market, is. Software you can copy in a weekend; a trusted local integrated operator you cannot.

## On traction & validation

**Q: You're pre-revenue. What proof do you have that people will pay?**
A: *(Honest, staged.)* Today: a working visualizer and a pre-launch interest campaign. We're explicit that the current deposits are refundable, so they signal interest, not committed intent — we're converting that into a real intent instrument and running 15–20 structured customer interviews now. We'd rather show you a smaller *honest* number than a big misleading one. *[Upgrade this answer as G1/G2 close.]*

**Q: Aren't your "backers" just a vanity metric?**
A: If we presented refundable ~$1 deposits as demand, yes — and we won't. That's why we're re-engineering the instrument to measure committed intent before we cite it as traction. Naming that ourselves is the point: we hold our own metrics to the same trust standard we sell.

## On economics

**Q: Do you actually make money on an order?**
A: *(Honest.)* Our modelled gross margin is `[__%]`, but the true per-order margin — after fabric, making-labour, the credited consultation, delivery and install — is being measured now from real order data (it's in our system, not yet exported). We won't quote it as fact until it's measured. A small-ticket custom item carrying free services can be thinner than it looks, and we're sizing that honestly before we scale it. *[Upgrade as G3 closes.]*

**Q: Free consultation, delivery, and install — doesn't that destroy your margin?**
A: It compresses it, and we treat that as a live question, not a settled one. The strategic bet is that bundling *certainty* is what lets us escape the curtain price war and charge a premium — but we're measuring whether the premium covers the bundle, and we'll re-price the unit (per finished window, all-in) if it doesn't.

## On the team & market

**Q: Why you two?**
A: The problem needs taste *and* engineering. Rose is the designer and the customer trust; Simon is the engineer and the product. Designer + technical co-founder is exactly the pairing, and it unlocks both women-founder and technical-founder capital.

**Q: Why Nyeri and not Nairobi?**
A: Lower cost base, an underserved growing market, and a genuine inclusion/jobs story for impact and government capital. We can serve Central Kenya and Nairobi from here; the location is an edge, not a limitation.

## On the ask & the raise

**Q: Why are you raising non-dilutive/grant money instead of equity?**
A: Because at our stage equity is the most expensive capital we could take — a low pre-launch valuation permanently over-dilutes for the least cash. Non-dilutive capital funds the proof that raises our eventual valuation. We'll raise equity when we've proven the economics and are funding the H2/H3 climb, not to survive H1.

**Q: What does this specific money buy?**
A: A named milestone — `[e.g. converting validated interest into paying demand + the first production run]`. We don't raise in the abstract; every shilling is tagged to a milestone that unlocks the next, cheaper round of capital.

**Q: What are the risks / what keeps you up at night?**
A: Runway timing (grants are slow), the capacity ceiling of a founder-bound service, and unproven unit economics — all of which we're actively de-risking (see our readiness plan). We'd rather tell you our risk register than pretend we don't have one.

## Objection-handling principles

1. **Concede the true gap, then show the plan.** "You're right that X isn't proven — here's exactly how we're proving it by [date]." Beats denial every time.
2. **Never inflate to impress.** One caught exaggeration costs more than ten honest weaknesses, especially for a company whose entire thesis is trust.
3. **Reframe to the real moat** when pushed on the copyable parts (software, visualizer).
4. **Log every new objection here** after each conversation, with the answer that landed.
