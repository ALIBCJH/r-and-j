# Phase 02b — Customer Intelligence Repository

**R&J Interiors · Company Brain**
Status: Living repository · Version 1.0 · 2026-07-09
Owner: Customer Intelligence (standing) · Review cadence: refresh each time new primary or secondary evidence lands; re-verify time-sensitive stats quarterly

> **What this document is.** A repository of *external, cited evidence* about the people R&J serves — Kenyan homeowners buying curtains, interior design, and home transformations. It is the secondary-research companion to [Phase 02 — Customer](phase-02-customer.md). Where Phase 02 is a **hypothesis** document built from the shipped product and founder intuition (its own §2 grades it **C**), this repository goes to the outside world — Reddit, Quora, Kenyan journalism, blogs, forums, industry reports — and asks: *does the evidence support the bets?* It upgrades some of Phase 02's grade-C guesses toward grade-B, **contradicts the objection ranking in one important way**, and leaves the single most consequential question (willingness-to-pay a premium) still unanswered.
>
> **The honesty rule that governs this document.** Secondary evidence is **not** primary evidence. Nothing here is a quote from an R&J customer; R&J has still run zero interviews (Phase 02 §12 is still the real deliverable). Every insight below is graded:
> - **Grade A** — strong, direct, multiple *independent Kenyan* sources.
> - **Grade B** — triangulated but indirect, or a global/US/UK analogue that transfers on physics or universal psychology (labelled as such).
> - **Grade C** — thin, single-source, or commercially-interested.
>
> Treat Grade B/C findings as **hypotheses to confirm locally**, never as established Kenyan fact. This grading is deliberately conservative because the sources skew toward marketing blogs; a claim survived only where independent Kenyan journalism or established science corroborated it.

## 0. Method & Provenance

**How this was built.** A fan-out research harness ran 5 search angles → fetched 19 sources → extracted 83 falsifiable claims → put the top 25 through 3-vote adversarial verification (a claim needed ≥2 of 3 independent agents to *confirm* it to survive). **15 claims confirmed, 10 refuted.** The survivors are synthesised below.

**Source spine (independent, non-commercial, Kenyan — the load-bearing ones):** Daily Nation, The Standard, Tuko.co.ke, BuyRentKenya, Anti-Counterfeit Authority, TransUnion Kenya. Commercially-interested sources (curtain sellers, design firms, AR vendors) were retained *only* where one of the above or established science corroborated them.

**⚠️ The Kenya-First gap (read before trusting any regret finding).** The strongest, most direct evidence for the *curtain-specific* regrets (colour under room light, shrinkage, narrow panels, cheap headers) is **global/US/UK** — Reddit-derived design blogs and US retailers. There is **no Kenyan primary source** in this corpus quoting a Kenyan buyer regretting a specific curtain purchase — no r/Kenya thread, no Kenyan Facebook-group post, no TikTok. Those regrets transfer to Kenya on *physics* (metamerism, fibre shrinkage are universal) and *measurement facts*, which is why they're Grade B not A — but they are **hypotheses for R&J's own interviews to confirm**, not proven Kenyan behaviour. By contrast, the *trust/distrust* and *status/diaspora* findings **are** directly Kenyan and multi-source (Grade A).

**🚫 Do not cite these — they FAILED verification.** Several attractive statistics were refuted and must never appear in R&J copy or decks:
- "AR reduces returns 25–40% / 35% (Wayfair) / raises confidence 85%→98% (IKEA)" — all refuted.
- "42% would pay more for a product they can view in 3D/AR" — refuted.
- Specific Kenyan curtain price bands (the "Ksh 1,000–5,000 ready-made / 3,000–15,000 custom" figures) — refuted; do not quote as fact.
- "Buyers regret prioritising aesthetics over function" — refuted as a framing.

Use only the **qualitative** version of the visualization benefit ("reduces surprises, raises confidence"), never a fabricated percentage. This protects Principle 2 (trust > growth) and Principle 6 (never adopt tech for fashion): we will not sell certainty tech on numbers that aren't real.

## 1. Executive Summary — What the Outside Evidence Says

**The Kenyan residential interior/curtain buyer is governed by trust anxiety on an effectively irreversible, non-returnable purchase.** The single best-evidenced fear is *not* aesthetic — it is **"I will pay a maker who then under-delivers, disappears after the deposit, changes the price midway, or hands over something different from what we agreed."** This is documented across independent Kenyan media on fundis, online sellers, and diaspora developers, and it is Grade A. The aesthetic regrets (wrong colour in my light, wrong length, cheap-looking) are real and emotionally charged but are **Grade B global-analogues** in this corpus.

**This produces the most important strategic correction to Phase 02.** Phase 02 §8 ranked the objections as (1) *"will it look like that in MY room?"* then (2) *"will you deliver what we agreed / disappear with my deposit?"* **The Kenyan evidence inverts the confidence ordering:** the deliver-what-we-agreed / deposit-and-disappear objection has the *strongest and most directly Kenyan* evidence; the visualization objection is real but rests on global analogues. R&J's Brain leans heavily on the "her-room preview" thesis — the evidence says the *legible-trust* thesis is the better-proven one, and the visualizer earns its place only if it also functions as a **trust instrument** (a visual contract), not merely a pretty preview.

**The mechanism that ties it together: buyers already run verification rituals.** Before paying, serious Kenyan buyers demand a physical location, an official M-Pesa Paybill (a personal number reads as a scam), and a *recent raw dated photo of the actual product* (not a stock image). This is Grade A. **The seller who pre-empts the buyer's checklist wins the deal.** R&J's entire sales flow should be built to hand the buyer their own trust-checklist, already ticked.

**The emotional engine is pride/arrival**, strongest in the diaspora — the customer simultaneously most willing to invest and most afraid of being scammed remotely (Grade A). This is a distinct, high-value, under-served segment.

**What the evidence could NOT settle — and this matters most.** Whether the fear of getting it wrong is strong enough that Kenyan buyers **pay a premium** for certainty (in-home swatches, 3D/AR preview, guarantees), or whether they treat it as a survivable annoyance they'd rather absorb than pay to avoid, **remains unresolved.** No Kenyan willingness-to-pay evidence surfaced, and the global stats that would have implied it were refuted. **Phase 02 §7's painkiller-vs-vitamin question is still open, still grade-C, and still a one-way door (Phase 01 §13). Do not set premium pricing on this repository.**

## 2. The Repository — Insights by Dimension

Each insight: **Evidence → Why it matters → Opportunity for R&J**, with an evidence grade and the adversarial vote.

### 2.1 — PAIN POINTS

**P1 · Custom-curtain timeline uncertainty pushes hot leads to ready-made. — Grade B (Kenyan, single neutral source) · vote 3-0**
- **Evidence.** A neutral Kenyan property-portal buying guide states that with custom curtains you "never know how long it will take for the tailor to complete stitching them"; advertised turnarounds vary widely (one maker cites 5–10 days). (BuyRentKenya, *How To Buy Curtains in Kenya*; Kalus.)
- **Why it matters.** Timeline uncertainty is the *one* concrete advantage ready-made has over custom. A time-pressured buyer (moving, hosting) abandons custom not on price but on *"will it be ready in time?"* — a fear R&J can neutralise.
- **Opportunity.** Publish and *guarantee* a firm turnaround ("ready in X days, or a discount"), and offer an express lane for movers/hosts. Removes ready-made's only edge and reinforces the promise-kept brand (Phase 01 §12 "Promise-Kept Rate").

**P2 · Renovation logistics (material-cost volatility, inconsistent labour, permits) sour larger jobs. — Grade B (Kenyan, split) · vote 2-1**
- **Evidence.** "The cost of construction materials rises every month, skilled labour is inconsistent, and navigating local permits can feel like a maze." (Prime House Interiors; corroborated by ProBuilders Kenya, Zao.) Scoping caveat flagged in verification: this is *general-construction* pain, only partly R&J's soft-furnishing niche.
- **Why it matters.** For R&J's fuller interior-design/transformation jobs (not standalone curtains), these practical anxieties feed the distrust of contractors that dominates §2.2.
- **Opportunity.** On larger jobs, absorb the complexity the customer fears: transparent sourcing/pricing, vetted installers, permit guidance. **Lower priority than the curtain wedge** — relevant to Phase 06/Phase 09 own-the-room expansion, not the entry product.

### 2.2 — OBJECTIONS / DISTRUST *(the strongest-evidenced dimension — Grade A)*

**O1 · The deepest objection: "deposit-and-disappear" / "delivered-differs-from-agreed." — Grade A (multi-source Kenyan) · vote 3-0**
- **Evidence.** Multiple independent Kenyan outlets, unanimous: fundis "take a deposit and gleefully offer a completion date [that] rarely ever comes," disappear after pocketing deposits, "inflate costs, or deliver substandard work." (ProBuilders Kenya; The Standard, *Put your fundi on contract*; Tuko, *7 Lies Kenyan Fundis Tell Clients*; BuyRentKenya, *Building in Kenya: 7 Mistakes*.)
- **Why it matters.** **This is the #1 barrier between R&J and a sale on any high-value, pay-upfront job — and it is the best-evidenced finding in the entire corpus.** It outranks the aesthetic "her-room" objection on evidence quality. It is the customer-side proof of Phase 01's core thesis: R&J sells *trust*; the curtain is the occasion.
- **Opportunity.** **Productize trust as the headline promise.** Staged/milestone payments instead of a large upfront deposit; a written itemised quote with a *no-change price guarantee*; a firm completion date with a self-imposed penalty/discount if missed; a visible physical premises. Make **"we don't take your money and vanish"** an explicit, marketed commitment. *This should arguably lead the brand ahead of the visualizer.*

**O2 · Buyers are primed to distrust IG / WhatsApp / Facebook sellers specifically. — Grade A (multi-source Kenyan) · vote 3-0**
- **Evidence.** Non-delivery ("paying and it is never delivered") and counterfeits are Kenya's dominant online-shopping fraud categories. Counterfeit "is the most popular form of online shopping fraud"; ~1 in 5 Kenyan products are counterfeit and 31%+ of buyers have bought counterfeits online (Anti-Counterfeit Authority); 39% of Kenyan digital-fraud victims were hit by third-party-seller scams (TransUnion, 2026). (Saidia.co.ke; LawGuide.co.ke; Somo Group; ACA; TransUnion.)
- **Why it matters.** R&J's most likely discovery channels (Instagram, WhatsApp) are **the exact channels buyers have been taught to fear.** Every prepayment ask on those channels fights a headwind of learned suspicion. *(Time-sensitivity: the 2026 fraud stats will drift; re-verify quarterly.)*
- **Opportunity.** Over-signal legitimacy on every channel: official M-Pesa **Paybill** (never a personal number), a verifiable mapped physical shop in Nyeri/Nairobi, real reviews, and a money-back or escrow-style assurance that directly neutralises non-delivery fear.

**O3 · The meta-point.** Every surviving objection is a *trust* objection, not a *feature* objection — exactly as Phase 02 §8 predicted. The evidence confirms that half of Phase 02, and sharpens it: in Kenya, the trust objection is not just present, it is the **best-documented buyer emotion in this category.**

### 2.3 — DECISION-MAKING PATTERNS

**D1 · Buyers de-risk with concrete verification rituals before paying. — Grade A (multi-source Kenyan) · vote 3-0**
- **Evidence.** "Insist on them sharing their location"; a registered Paybill "shows they take their business seriously"; "ask the IG or FB store to send you a recent raw pic … not a screenshot or download" (sometimes verified against the day's newspaper). (Saidia.co.ke; ScamBusterKE; Bond Advocates LLP; Tuko case of a buyer requesting in-shop photos.)
- **Why it matters.** These are the *literal steps* a serious buyer takes. Discovery and choice in this market run on a checklist, not on branding. A seller who **pre-empts the checklist** removes the buyer's work and their fear at once.
- **Opportunity.** Build the sales flow to hand the buyer their checklist pre-ticked: a Paybill, a mapped showroom, and *fresh, dated, real* photos/short video of the specific fabric and the actual in-progress work. **Turn the buyer's due-diligence ritual into R&J's pitch.** This also reframes the visualizer: its most defensible job is as *proof of the real thing*, not a rendered fantasy.

**D2 · Trust is won by a structured, *visual* process over "vague verbal promises." — Grade B (Kenyan firms + global, one 404) · vote 3-0**
- **Evidence.** Nairobi design firms position a clear process — "site visit, concept and 3D render, then execution — instead of vague verbal promises." "When a client can see their space in photorealistic 3D … trust fundamentally changes … before anything is ordered." (interiordesigners.co.ke [URL now 404s]; VirtualSpaces; Cedreo/ArchiCGI; primedrawings.co.ke; Prestige Bluestar.) *Grade B: SEO/marketing-toned and one dead link, but triangulated including Kenyan firms.*
- **Why it matters.** "Seeing it before I buy" is simultaneously an anxiety-reducer *and* a trust signal — the render functions as a **visual contract** that reduces "that's not what I pictured" disputes. This is the evidence bridge between Phase 02's her-room thesis and the O1 trust thesis: **the preview earns its keep as a trust instrument.**
- **Opportunity.** Make a visual preview a standard, *marketed* step — a 3D render, an AR room mock-up, or at minimum a physical mood-board + real material samples staged in the customer's room. Position it explicitly as *"so we both agree on the result before anyone pays for it."* **Caveat:** per Phase 02 §0.2.1, the shipped Studio re-tints stock scenes and does **not** yet composite onto the customer's photo — so today R&J markets this capability ahead of delivering it. The evidence *raises* the value of closing that gap; it does not license claiming it's already closed.

### 2.4 — EMOTIONAL DRIVERS

**E1 · The home is a pride / "I've arrived" status signal, with heavy social weight when guests visit. — Grade A (Kenyan, current) · vote 3-0**
- **Evidence.** Kenyan homeownership is framed as "a symbol of success, social status, legacy, and cultural pride"; diaspora makeovers are sold as "modern, luxurious homes designed to reflect your hard work abroad and your pride at home." (Prestige Bluestar; Tuko, *Changing Home*; Willstone Homes; BuyRentKenya.)
- **Why it matters.** Buyers aren't buying curtains — they're buying *how the home makes them feel and look to others.* This is the social job from Phase 02 §3, and it powers referral (the proud home is the advertisement).
- **Opportunity.** Market the emotional payoff — pride when hosting, a home that reflects who you've become — using aspirational-but-attainable *Kenyan-home* imagery (the real-photo direction R&J already shipped in the proof strip). Make before/after reveals and "guest-ready" styling the sellable outcome, and the natural referral moment.

**E2 · Diaspora buyers are the most fear-driven *and* most motivated segment. — Grade A (multi-source Kenyan) · vote 3-0**
- **Evidence.** "Managing a project from abroad can be overwhelming, with risks of poor workmanship, cost inflation, and stalled timelines"; Daily Nation documents developers scamming diaspora homebuyers; homes are framed as "love letters" and "the most important sign of success." Remittances are at record levels (~USD 5B). (Prestige Bluestar; Daily Nation; Kenya Insights; Tuko; allAfrica/Capital FM.)
- **Why it matters.** This customer has the highest willingness to invest *and* the highest fear of being scammed remotely — the exact intersection where "sell certainty" is worth the most. They cannot run the §2.3 verification rituals in person, so they need R&J to run the rituals *for* them.
- **Opportunity.** Build an explicit **diaspora offer**: remote consultations, milestone photo/video updates, verified progress reporting, transparent fixed pricing, a named local point of contact. Sell *peace of mind* to buyers who can't supervise. (Note: this is a distinct segment/JTBD; scope carefully against the residential-homeowner focus — it's a homeowner sub-segment, not the deferred B2B customer.)

**E3 · The underlying psychology: loss aversion on an irreversible choice. — Grade B (global, universal) · vote 3-0 (mechanism)**
- **Evidence.** Consumer-psychology sources: fear of a wrong decision drives buyers to delay or default to the status quo; loss aversion makes avoiding a loss more motivating than an equivalent gain. *(Global-analogue; universal cognitive mechanism, but not Kenya-specific.)*
- **Why it matters.** Explains *why* the trust and regret findings bite so hard on a non-returnable purchase — and warns that the same fear can produce **inaction** ("I'll do it later"), the silent sale-killer from Phase 02 §8.
- **Opportunity.** Reduce the *perceived* irreversibility (previews, guarantees, staged commitment) rather than pushing manufactured urgency — which would violate Principle 2. The antidote to "I'll do it later" is lowered risk, not higher pressure.

### 2.5 — REGRETS *(emotionally sharpest dimension — but Grade B, global; confirm locally)*

**R1 · The #1 regret is colour that reads wrong in the buyer's actual room (metamerism). — Grade B (global-analogue; physics universal) · vote 3-0**
- **Evidence.** "They look great in the day, but at night with my lamps on, they look GREEN!"; one panel "turned green against her wall paint … the other looked softly warm under the room's evening lamps." Grounded in documented colour science (illuminant metamerism). (Three Girls, *I Read 1,000+ Reddit Threads*; NICETOWN; TextileGlossary; HunterLab; Curtarra.)
- **Why it matters.** The most concrete, repeated, emotionally charged regret in the corpus — and *precisely* the anxiety that makes "seeing it before I buy" worth paying for. It is the strongest external support for Phase 02's her-room preview thesis. **But note the honest ceiling:** it is global evidence, and it supports *conversion/trust*, not (on this evidence) a price premium.
- **Opportunity.** Make large physical swatches free and standard — *"view this fabric in your own light, day and night, before you commit"* — and/or a 3D/AR room preview. This converts the dominant regret into R&J's differentiator. **The physical-swatch version is cheaper, more trustworthy, and more immediately shippable than AR** — and, unlike a render, it cannot be accused of flattering the product.

**R2 · Wrong size — too short (shrinkage) and too narrow (measuring glass, not rod). — Grade B (global; measurement physics universal) · vote 3-0 / 2-1**
- **Evidence.** "I washed my linen curtains … They shrank 6 inches!"; buyers "measure the window width but not the total rod width" so panels "looked too narrow"; improper sizing cited as a top cause of window-treatment returns. (Three Girls; NICETOWN; Emily Henderson; Livingetc.)
- **Why it matters.** Sizing errors are irreversible once fabric is cut/hemmed, and they're the #1 mistake across design authorities. This is the functional case for *made-to-measure over ready-made and over DIY-online.*
- **Opportunity.** Lead the made-to-measure service with *"we measure for you, on-site,"* and educate on rod-vs-glass width and fabric shrinkage. Turns a universal regret into the reason to choose R&J's custom over a cheaper ready-made panel.

**R3 · Grommet/ring-header curtains are regretted as "cheap, like a shower curtain." — Grade C (single Reddit anecdote + US design opinion) · vote 2-1**
- **Evidence.** Reddit user: grommets "look cheap, like a shower curtain," forcing "wide, messy waves rather than tailored pleats." (Three Girls; House Digest; RoomFlip; The Savvy Heart.) *Grade C: one anecdote amplified by US design blogs; weakest survivor.*
- **Why it matters.** Buyers who spend on custom want the result to *signal* quality/status (see E1), not read as mass-produced — but this is the thinnest finding here; don't over-index.
- **Opportunity.** Offer and gently upsell pinch-pleat/tailored headers as the "looks bespoke, not off-the-shelf" option, with side-by-side before/after. **Verify local taste first** — Kenyan header preferences may differ; do not assume Reddit's taste is Nairobi's.

### 2.6 — DESIRED OUTCOMES

**DO1 · A perfect fit ready-made cannot deliver. — Grade A/B (Kenyan corroboration present) · vote 3-0**
- **Evidence.** Custom "eliminate[s] gaps, uneven lengths, and poor fabric drape"; Kenyan BuyRentKenya notes ready-made "might not fit windows and would require some alterations"; The Standard confirms wrong-length curtains "look ugly." (Kalus; BuyRentKenya; dotcomblinds; Standard/Evewoman.)
- **Why it matters.** Fit is the *tangible functional payoff* that justifies choosing and paying for custom — and it's the antidote to R2. Includes genuine Kenyan corroboration, so stronger than the pure-regret findings.
- **Opportunity.** Frame the whole value proposition around *"fits your window exactly — no gaps, no light leak, no puddling,"* backed by on-site measurement. Position custom as **the safe choice, not the risky/expensive one** — reframing away from the price war (Phase 01 §6).

**DO2 · Certainty tech (AR/3D "see it in your space") raises confidence — qualitatively. — Grade B (global; stats refuted) · vote 2-1**
- **Evidence.** Visualising an item in one's own space prevents surprises — "a sofa that clashes with the walls or a rug that feels undersized." Mechanism corroborated qualitatively (IKEA Place, Shopify 3D/AR). **BUT** the specific percentages (Wayfair 35%, IKEA 85%→98%, "42% pay more," 25–40%) were **all refuted in verification.** (imagine.io; Threekit; Shopify; IKEA Place.)
- **Why it matters.** On *non-returnable* custom curtains, the mechanism converts to purchase *confidence*, not just fewer returns — it answers the "will this look right?" fear (R1). This is the strategic case for the visualizer **and its limit**: the benefit is real and qualitative; the ROI numbers that would justify heavy investment are not trustworthy.
- **Opportunity.** Pilot an AR/3D preview *or* the cheaper, more-trustworthy physical-sample-in-your-light equivalent as R&J's signature "see it before you commit" feature. **Sell the qualitative confidence, never a fabricated stat.** Given O1 (trust is the real objection) and the refuted stats, the *swatch-in-your-light + visual-contract render* is a better first bet than a heavy AR build (aligns Principle 5: solve the real problem before building software).

### 2.7 — PURCHASE TRIGGERS

**T1 · Diaspora homecoming / building-from-abroad is a documented, high-value trigger. — Grade A (Kenyan) · vote 3-0**
- **Evidence.** Record remittances funding homes framed as pride/arrival signals; diaspora makeover services exist precisely because this trigger is real and monetised. (Prestige Bluestar; Tuko; Capital FM; Daily Nation.) *(Cross-ref E2.)*
- **Why it matters.** A *named, observable* trigger is more acquirable than a demographic (Phase 02 §4). Diaspora building is the one trigger this corpus proves.
- **Opportunity.** Target the diaspora trigger explicitly (see E2's offer). It is R&J's most evidence-backed acquisition wedge beyond referral.

**T2 · Moving / hosting compress the timeline and favour whoever can guarantee delivery. — Grade B (Kenyan, indirect) · vote 3-0 (via P1)**
- **Evidence.** The Kenyan buying guide ties ready-made-vs-custom to life events ("if you are moving out or want to host"), and timeline uncertainty (P1) is why time-pressured buyers compromise. (BuyRentKenya.)
- **Why it matters.** Move/host are the triggers where R&J's turnaround guarantee (P1) is the deciding factor, not price.
- **Opportunity.** Catch movers/hosts with the express-lane guarantee. **Gap:** the *relative frequency* of move vs marriage vs new-baby vs renovation triggers is **not** evidenced — a §12 interview question.

## 4. Highest-Confidence, Most Actionable Findings *(safe to act on now)*

1. **Make trust legible before you make the room beautiful.** The best-evidenced buyer fear (Grade A) is deposit-and-disappear / delivered-differs. Staged payments, a no-change itemised quote, a firm date with a self-penalty, a real premises, an official Paybill — this is the headline promise. (O1, O2, D1)
2. **Hand the buyer their verification checklist, pre-ticked.** Paybill + mapped showroom + fresh dated real photos/video of the specific fabric and in-progress work. Turn due diligence into the pitch. (D1)
3. **Free large swatch "in your own light" as the signature certainty feature** — cheaper, more trustworthy, and more shippable than AR, and it directly kills the #1 regret. (R1, DO2)
4. **Guarantee turnaround; run an express lane for movers/hosts** — removes ready-made's only advantage. (P1, T2)
5. **Build a diaspora peace-of-mind offer** — remote consult, milestone video proof, fixed pricing, named local contact — for the segment most willing to invest and most afraid. (E2, T1)
6. **Sell certainty tech on qualitative confidence, never on the refuted percentages.** (DO2 caveat)

## 5. The Biggest Evidence Gaps — What Only R&J's Own Interviews Can Close

These are the load-bearing unknowns this secondary research **could not** resolve. They map directly to Phase 02 §12.

1. **Willingness-to-pay a premium for certainty** — the single most consequential unknown. No Kenyan evidence; global stats refuted. *Test by revealed preference (consultation-fee acceptance, non-refundable deposit uptake, premium-fabric mix), not by asking.* (Phase 02 §7, §12-C)
2. **Actual discovery-channel mix for Nyeri/Nairobi** — referral vs Instagram vs WhatsApp vs Jumia vs physical shop. We know buyers *distrust* online sellers; we did not *quantify* how R&J's real customers find R&J. (Phase 02 §10, §12-B "how did you hear about us?")
3. **Which life-event triggers dominate, and their relative frequency** — diaspora and general "arrival" pride are proven; move/marriage/new-baby/hosting/renovation are not ranked for R&J's market. (Phase 02 §12-A)
4. **Whether the global curtain regrets manifest the same way in Kenya** — or whether Kenya-specific regrets (tailor overcharging, delivered-differs on custom, local fabric/lighting quirks) dominate instead. Every R1–R3 finding is a hypothesis until a Kenyan buyer says it. (Phase 02 §12-A, the "killer question")

**Exit criterion.** This repository upgrades from "secondary-evidence" to "triangulated with primary" only when Phase 02 §12's ≥10 interviews test findings O1, R1, DO2 and the four gaps above against real R&J customers. Until then: **Grade A findings are safe to build on; Grade B/C findings are hypotheses to confirm; willingness-to-pay is untouched.**
